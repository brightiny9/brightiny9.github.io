Two big companies shipped "proactive" personal agents within a few weeks of each other: Meta's Muse (September 2026) and OpenAI's dots (announced September 29, 2026). A third, Grok Bot from xAI, keeps coming up in the same threads. I lined them up on what a small maker would want to know: who can use it, what it costs, and what it does.

The short version: the official pages say what these agents are *for* and almost nothing about what they *cost*. This post is a ledger of what's confirmed and what isn't, not a review. I haven't used either product.

## What the official sources say

**Muse (Meta).** Meta says it's meant to do things, not just answer: sending emails, booking travel and negotiating on your behalf. Other details from the page:

- Rolling out in the United States on iOS, Android and muse.ai. AI glasses are "coming soon."
- It runs on what Meta calls a Muse Secure VM and is powered by Muse Spark, "Meta's most capable model to date."
- It connects to apps like WhatsApp, Instagram and email, and can pay through Link by Stripe, with purchase protections.

Source: [Meta newsroom](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/09/introducing-muse-personal-ai-agent/), read October 9, 2026.

**dots (OpenAI).** I could only read OpenAI's own RSS summary, because the announcement page blocked my browser (HTTP 403). It says: "Dots by OpenAI are proactive assistants that can keep working across complex projects and everyday tasks. Learn how dots help you stay in control while work moves forward." That's all I can vouch for.

Source: [OpenAI news feed](https://openai.com/news/rss.xml), entry dated September 29, 2026, read October 9, 2026.

## What I can't tell you

- What either product costs. Neither source I could read gives a price.
- Whether dots is available outside the US, or which plans include it.
- How well any of them works. Nothing here is a test.

## My take (opinion, not fact)

Both announcements describe the same shift: from an assistant you ask to an agent that keeps going on its own. Two things follow for small makers.

First, the permission model is the product. An agent that sends email and pays for things needs trust more than it needs a clever model. Muse's pitch leans on security and purchase protection, and dots' pitch leans on "stay in control." I'd judge both on how easy it is to see what they did and undo it. Neither source lets me check that.

Second, if you build tools that people ask an assistant to run, this is a distribution question. If the default assistant starts acting on its own, your product needs to be something it can reach and use. That's a guess from the product descriptions, and I haven't tested it.
