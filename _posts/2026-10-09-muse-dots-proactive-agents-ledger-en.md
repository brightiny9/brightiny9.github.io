Two big companies shipped "proactive" personal agents within a few weeks of each other: Meta's Muse (September 2026) and OpenAI's dots (announced September 29, 2026). A third, Grok Bot from xAI, keeps coming up in the same threads. I tried to line them up on the facts a small maker would care about: who can use it, what it costs, and what it actually does.

The short version: the official pages tell you what these agents are *for* and very little about what they *cost*. I also couldn't read everything. I'll mark that as I go.

## What the official sources say

**Muse (Meta).** Meta's announcement calls it "a secure, private personal AI agent that proactively helps with people's goals and suggests ideas." It's meant to do things, not just answer: Meta lists sending emails, booking travel and negotiating on your behalf. Details from the page:

- Rolling out in the United States on iOS, Android and muse.ai. AI glasses are "coming soon."
- It runs on what Meta calls a Muse Secure VM and is powered by Muse Spark, "Meta's most capable model to date."
- It connects to apps like WhatsApp, Instagram and email, and can pay through Link by Stripe, with purchase protections.
- Pricing: "free for most of what people need, with subscription plans for people who want to do more." No plan names, prices or usage limits are given.

Source: [Meta newsroom](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/09/introducing-muse-personal-ai-agent/), read October 9, 2026.

**dots (OpenAI).** I could only read OpenAI's own RSS summary, because the announcement page blocked my browser (HTTP 403). The summary says: "Dots by OpenAI are proactive assistants that can keep working across complex projects and everyday tasks. Learn how dots help you stay in control while work moves forward." That's all I can vouch for. Which plans get dots, what it costs and what the limits are: not in the summary, so I don't know.

Source: [OpenAI news feed](https://openai.com/news/rss.xml), entry dated September 29, 2026, read October 9, 2026.

**Grok Bot (xAI).** I couldn't open xAI's page (also 403), and I have no first-party source. I'm leaving it out of the comparison rather than repeating second-hand claims.

## What I can't tell you

- What either product costs. Neither source I could read gives a price.
- Whether Muse or dots is available outside the US, other than Muse's US-first rollout.
- How well any of them works. I haven't used them, and nothing here is a test.

## My take (opinion, not fact)

Both announcements describe the same shift: from an assistant you ask to an agent that keeps going on its own. Two things follow for small makers.

First, the permission model is the product. An agent that sends email and pays for things needs trust more than it needs a clever model. Muse's pitch leans on security and purchase protection, and dots' pitch leans on "stay in control." I'd judge both on how easy it is to see what they did and undo it. Neither page lets me check that.

Second, if you build tools that people ask an assistant to run, this is a distribution question. If the default assistant starts acting on its own, your product needs to be something it can reach and use. That's a guess from the product descriptions, and I haven't tested it.
